# Daybook UI research — agency work

**Researched:** 23 November 2026. **Scope:** first-party design studio case studies for named finance products. Studio outcomes and metrics are self-reported; the useful evidence here is the documented design decision, not a claim that the same result will occur in Daybook. These examples are inspiration, not screens or assets to copy.

## 1. 350life × Cashews — one meaningful number

[350life's Cashews case study](https://350life.com/work/cashews) describes a personal-finance app that opens on a single “safe to spend” figure. The studio says it tested multiple wireframes and prototypes to decide how much detail should sit around that number; its case study shows a spending dashboard, recent transactions, and deeper insight screens. The product has bank connections and predictive calculations, which Daybook does not.

**For Daybook:** make **today's spending** the most prominent figure, with this month's total close by. Give the recent expense list enough visibility to explain the number. **Avoid:** copying a “safe to spend” amount, budget meter, or predictive chart; Daybook has no data to justify those claims.

## 2. Arounda × Kinves — hierarchy before decoration

[Arounda's Kinves case study](https://arounda.agency/works/kinves) describes a personal-finance home screen that gives one figure clear priority and makes common tasks directly accessible. The studio says it assigned a primary purpose to each screen before choosing colors, type, or visual emphasis. Its numerical usability outcomes are agency-reported.

**For Daybook:** make **Add expense** directly accessible from Home and keep each screen focused on one purpose: entry, summary, history, or settings. **Avoid:** Kinves's transfer and balance patterns, since Daybook tracks manually entered spending rather than bank-account operations.

## 3. Clay × Lulo Bank — restrained motion and visual cues

[Clay's Lulo Bank case study](https://clay.global/work/lulo-bank) describes a mobile banking interface with simple navigation, custom iconography, and small interaction animations. The studio says its visual details were meant to make information easier to perceive, while maintaining a straightforward interface.

**For Daybook:** use subtle confirmation motion after saving and a small, clear status change when an expense syncs. Use consistent icons only where they improve recognition. **Avoid:** heavy animated dashboards, illustrations on every screen, and bank-card imagery; they add weight without helping expense entry.

## 4. Daylight × EarnIn — useful motion around money over time

[Daylight's EarnIn case study](https://www.daylightdesign.com/projects/earnin) describes a redesign of home/navigation and exploration of cash-flow visualizations. The team says it prototyped motion studies and tested different views with users before choosing what shipped. EarnIn is a wage-access app with broader money flows than Daybook.

**For Daybook:** if a chart is added, let it answer a concrete question such as “Which categories account for this month's spending?” A simple bar list is enough for v1; animate only transitions that help a user see a change. **Avoid:** decorative charts or implied income/cash-flow calculations that Daybook does not yet track.

## 5. Humbleteam × Afternoon — one job per card

[Humbleteam's Afternoon case study](https://humbleteam.com/work/afternoon) describes a financial-adviser product built from a strong accent color, large light surfaces, and cards that each hold one task. It also stresses consistent parts and clear, plain prompts across its platform and mobile app. This is an adviser tool, not a consumer expense tracker.

**For Daybook:** a compact opening card can ask “Add today's spending?” with one clear action and a dismiss option. Use one restrained accent color for active controls and keep the rest of the UI quiet. **Avoid:** reproducing its orange brand, AI assistant, or enterprise dashboard.

## Recommended synthesis for Daybook

Use an **editorial expense journal** direction: large readable money figures, a clear daily-entry action, sparse surfaces, a date-led history, and small accents for sync state. Combine Cashews's single focal number, Kinves's task hierarchy, Lulo's restrained feedback, and Afternoon's one-job cards. Keep Daybook's own wording, iconography, palette, and layouts.

One possible design system for exploration: warm near-white background, ink-dark text, deep teal primary action, a muted clay highlight for prompts, rounded but compact cards, tabular numerals for amounts, and a chart limited to monthly category bars. This palette and component set are **Daybook proposals**, not attributes claimed for the cited case studies. Check contrast and dark-mode behavior before implementation.
